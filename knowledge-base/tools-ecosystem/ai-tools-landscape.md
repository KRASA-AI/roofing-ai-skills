### Voice AI — Home-Services-Specific Patterns
Generic voice AI and home-services-specific voice AI behave very differently in roofing workloads. Features that consistently matter for roofers:
- Job-type detection on first mention (storm damage vs. leak vs. re-roof vs. gutter) so the triage tier is set before the qualification questions even start
- Real-time calendar read/write to the estimator's actual calendar, so a booking is committed in the interaction rather than promised for callback
- Service-area filtering so out-of-area calls are politely redirected without clogging the estimator queue
- CRM write-back at call end, not as a nightly batch, so the same lead isn't worked twice
- Bilingual (English/Spanish) routing for storm-zone markets

### In-Shift Call Miss Pattern
The majority of missed inbound calls at a typical roofing shop happen during business hours while the office is stretched and crews are on roofs — not overnight. That means the biggest capacity gain from an AI voice agent is during-the-workday overflow handling, not only after-hours. Contractors shopping for an answering service should benchmark the vendor's same-day booking rate during peak hours, not just its 24/7 availability.

- **Pattern note:** Commercial roofing has a longer sales cycle and a narrower decision-maker pool than residential, which makes accurate targeting (right building + right human + right trigger) more important than outreach volume. ## Claims Workflow Integrations
Vertical integrations between measurement/estimating platforms and insurance-claims systems are accelerating:
- **Roofr ↔ Verisk (Xactimate ESX)** — Direct ESX file generation from the estimating platform eliminates manual roof redrawing in Xactimate and shortens claims turnaround
- **CompanyCam ↔ supplement workflows** — GPS-tagged photos flow from field documentation into supplement writing without manual export/import
- **Pattern note:** Integration consolidation is turning the CRM + measurement + estimating + claims stack into a single data spine. Contractors evaluating tools should weight integration depth more heavily than feature count.

## Agentic Property Intelligence (New — April 2026)
A new category emerged in late April 2026: property-intelligence platforms that expose their data as callable tools for AI agents rather than only as static reports or dashboards. The distinguishing pattern is **Model Context Protocol (MCP) integration** and **agent-to-agent interfaces** — an external AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, a roofer's internal agent) can directly invoke filter-and-retrieve operations against the platform's property dataset in natural-language workflows.

- **Eagleview Horizon (launched April 21, 2026)** — Agentic layer on top of the Eagleview One property dataset, exposing 20+ tools for property identification, filtering, scoring, and export through MCP. Marketed across insurance, construction/roofing, government, infrastructure, and property management. Roofing-specific use cases cited at launch include post-storm canvassing map generation filtered by roof age, roof material, roof size, and distance from a hail or wind swath — the same workflow that previously required stitching together three or four separate tools
- **Agent-to-agent pattern** — External AI systems (a roofer's CRM agent, a general-purpose assistant) can directly query the intelligence platform rather than a human operating the platform UI. This is the first time a roofing-relevant data vendor has shipped first-class agent invocation, and it makes automated post-storm territory planning and continuous canvassing-heatmap refresh structurally achievable
- **Market framing** — The launch targets a stated $1 trillion+ addressable opportunity spanning $28B roofing/construction, $1.05T P&C insurance direct premiums, and $120B property management — signalling that the roofing-specific use case sits inside a larger cross-industry platform play, which usually means the tool will stay funded and feature-rich but may not always prioritize roofing-first enhancements
- **Pattern note for roofers:** The legacy workflow for an older roof adjacent to a storm path took hours across multiple platforms; the agentic pattern collapses that to a single natural-language request and returns a drive-through-ready target list in minutes. The `storm-canvassing-prioritizer` skill operationalizes this pattern and works with or without platform access — agentic intelligence platforms are a force multiplier on top of the workflow, not a precondition
- **Watch items:** (a) whether competing property-intelligence vendors (Nearmap, CAPE Analytics, Zesty.ai) ship MCP interfaces on a similar timeline; (b) whether CRMs (JobNimbus, AccuLynx, ServiceTitan) expose agentic interfaces on the CRM side so a roofer's agent can act on CRM and property-intelligence data in a single session; (c) pricing transparency — launch posture is enterprise sales-led, which may price out solo operators and small shops for an initial period

## AI-Powered Lead Targeting & Buyer-Intent Segmentation (Pattern surfacing through 2026)
A second derivative of the property-intelligence layer is moving from generic "high-likelihood-of-replacement" lead lists toward segmenting by the type of work the contractor actually performs. The first reported example pairs aerial-condition modeling with homeowner-demographic and behavioral data and routes opportunities into separate replacement-prospect and rejuvenation-prospect buckets; reported relevance rates step from a 20–30% baseline to 75–85% when contractor work-type is explicitly modeled.

- **Reworked.ai (powered by Eagleview)** — Per-opportunity pricing on aerial-plus-demographic homeowner targeting; in early 2026 introduced an explicit replacement-vs-rejuvenation segmentation, with separate buyer-intent models for each. The contractor selects the work-type matching their service line, and the targeting engine returns prospects calibrated to that line rather than a single "needs-roof" list. Reported contractor outcomes include double-digit response-rate gains over generic mailers in the first cycle
- **Pattern note for roofers:** Specialty contractors (rejuvenation-only, metal-only, low-slope-only) have historically had to filter generic lead lists down by hand; per-work-type segmentation collapses that step and improves cost-per-opportunity. Mixed-service shops should be cautious about over-fitting — running both segments in parallel is usually safer than committing the full mail/canvassing budget to a single segment until the conversion rates clear two full seasons. Feed the priority score and outreach copy into the `predictive-lead-scorer` and `follow-up-sequence` skills so the segment context influences both ranking and tone (replacement prospects respond to lifecycle and financing language; rejuvenation prospects respond to maintenance-extension and warranty-preservation language)
- **Watch items:** (a) whether competing lead-data vendors expose comparable work-type segmentation (commercial vs. residential, repair vs. replacement vs. rejuvenation, asphalt vs. metal vs. tile); (b) whether segmentation accuracy degrades outside the original training markets as the model expands geographically; (c) whether the Eagleview-Horizon agentic layer eventually exposes the same segmentation as MCP-callable tools so a roofer's agent can request a typed lead set without leaving the chat

## Insurance-AI Landscape (Adversarial)
Insurance carriers now use AI against homeowners and, indirectly, against roofing contractors. Relevant vendors and dynamics:
- **CAPE Analytics, Nearmap, EagleView, Zesty.ai** — Aerial imagery vendors whose AI scores roofs for carriers. Outputs drive non-renewals, repair demands, and RCV-to-ACV conversions
- **Carrier AI decisions** — State Farm, Allstate, Farmers, and regional carriers are issuing non-renewals within 30–60 days based on aerial assessments homeowners never see
- **Predictive denial scoring on claim intake** — In addition to pre-renewal scoring, many carriers now run claims through a predictive denial model on intake, assigning a sub-two-second score that routes the claim into fast-approve, full-investigation, or automated-deny before a human adjuster is assigned. Supplement requests undergo a similar NLP triage before reaching a human reviewer
- **Common false positives** — Solar panels flagged as structural damage, neighbor imagery attributed to wrong parcel, seasonal shadows misclassified as defects, tree cover misread as moss
- **Roof-age thresholds tightening** — Many carriers moved triggers from 20 years to 15 years; some flag at 10 years
- **Contractor opportunity** — Homeowner-side counter-documentation is a new premium service ($250–$500 per inspection + appeal report). See `insurance-appeal-inspection-report` skill
- **Regulatory lever** — State insurance departments (notably NY DFS, Colorado DOI, California DOI, Florida OIR, Washington OIC) increasingly require explainability, disparate-impact testing, and human-review documentation on AI-driven adverse actions. Contractors can cite the applicable state rule in supplements and appeal packets to force the carrier to produce the explainability record — see `knowledge-base/regulations/insurance-ai-landscape.md` for the state-by-state reference and the citation pattern
- **Aerial-imagery-specific bulletins (Tennessee Bulletin 25-03, April 2026; Kentucky Bulletin 2026-01, March 2026)** — A second regulatory track is emerging that targets aerial imagery directly rather than AI in general. Tennessee requires that aerial images cannot be the sole basis for denial or non-renewal, must be recent and accurate, and must be disclosed to the homeowner with an opportunity to dispute. Kentucky requires that aerial imagery used in claims decisions be date-stamped within twelve months and accompanied by a written summary. Other high-storm states are expected to issue similar bulletins through 2026; cite the bulletin specifically when operating in TN or KY, and reference the pattern by analogy when operating in adjacent states. Full citation pattern is in `knowledge-base/regulations/insurance-ai-landscape.md`

## Homeowner-Facing Instant-Estimate Website Chatbots (New — April 2026)
A distinct lead-capture pattern surfaced in mid-April 2026 alongside the manufacturer-visualizer and voice-AI categories: a chatbot embedded directly on the contractor's own website that recognizes the visitor's roof from satellite imagery, computes square footage, and returns a price-range estimate inside the same conversation that captures contact information. The pattern sits between the static "request a quote" form and a full voice-AI receptionist — it converts anonymous web traffic into a qualified lead with a numeric anchor before the visitor leaves the page, and it fills the gap most roofing websites still have between marketing pages and CRM intake.

- **Roof'D AI website chatbot (launched April 12, 2026)** — The chatbot collects an address, pulls satellite imagery, outlines the roof, calculates square footage, and produces an estimate range tuned to the specific contractor's pricing, shingle brands, financing options, and service area. Two-tier pricing reported at launch (Starter and Pro), with a free trial. Each deployment is trained per contractor rather than served as a generic widget — the contractor's CRM receives the lead and the homeowner gets a same-session number rather than a callback promise. An early in-the-wild deployment was published in late April 2026 with a Central Illinois roofer routing the chatbot's leads into the shop CRM and using the on-site estimator (branded "RoofBot" in that deployment) as the always-on sales front door
- **Pattern note for roofers:** The structural difference vs. voice AI is channel — this captures web visitors who would not have called and would not have filled out a contact form, recovering an otherwise-lost slice of inbound. The structural difference vs. manufacturer visualizers (Owens Corning Design EyeQ, Renoworks AI) is ownership — the visitor stays on the contractor's domain and the lead is unambiguously the contractor's. The structural difference vs. carrier-side aerial imagery is intent — the homeowner is shopping, so the satellite measurement is welcomed rather than adversarial
- **Practical contractor angle:** Pair with the `lead-response-automator` skill so a chatbot-generated lead with a price anchor is followed up at the speed and tone the operator wants (typically faster than the AI's own follow-up cadence, because the homeowner already has a number in mind). Pair with the `predictive-lead-scorer` skill so chatbot-flow signals (depth of conversation, financing question asked, address inside a hail zone) feed the priority score
- **Watch items:** (a) whether AccuLynx, JobNimbus, ServiceTitan, or Roofr ship a comparable native widget that turns the contractor's CRM into the chatbot's backend rather than a paid add-on; (b) whether estimate-accuracy disclaimers harden into a state-level disclosure standard once enough homeowners receive an estimate range that diverges from the final contract; (c) competitive spread — additional purpose-built roofing entrants and any voice-AI vendors (AgentZap, Alivo, Dialzara) shipping a website-chat counterpart to their phone agent

## Manufacturer-Driven AI Tools (Pattern surfacing through 2026)
A distinct category from third-party AI tools: roofing manufacturers are now packaging AI capabilities as part of their contractor-network programs, creating a tier of AI tooling that is gated to contractors who carry the manufacturer's product line. The pattern matters because it changes the AI-stack cost calculation — a contractor who is already paying for manufacturer certification may have AI tooling included in that relationship, while a non-network competitor pays for an equivalent third-party tool out of pocket.

- **Owens Corning Design EyeQ® Roofing Visualizer (powered by Renoworks AI, October 2025)** — Hyper-realistic homeowner-facing visualizer hosted on the manufacturer site. Lets homeowners explore Owens Corning shingle styles, colors, and product combinations on a representative roof; the contractor receives the qualified lead generated through the experience. Useful for tier-up conversations on retail residential where the homeowner is choosing materials and the contractor is benchmarking the close rate against the standard mid-tier shingle selection
- **Owens Corning Roof Scout (3D-animated AI virtual sales agent, OC Contractor Network, 2026)** — A 3D-animated virtual character that walks homeowners through Owens Corning Total Protection Roofing System® education, product selection, transparent pricing and financing, and contract completion in a guided interactive video experience. The pattern is "round-the-clock e-commerce roof selling" — the homeowner can complete the buying decision after-hours without waiting for a sales rep, and the financing math is presented in-line so monthly-payment objections are surfaced earlier in the conversation. Practical contractor angle for OC Network members: pair with the `lead-response-automator` and `follow-up-sequence` skills so leads that complete or partially complete a Roof Scout flow are routed back through the shop's standard SLAs and the unfinished sessions are re-engaged within the company's normal cadence rather than re-queued only inside the OC pipeline
- **Renoworks enterprise visualizer expansion (eight new manufacturer/distributor visualizers; 15+ additional enterprise customers expected through Q1 2026)** — The cross-manufacturer pattern is that more shingle, metal, and accessory manufacturers will ship their own visualizers off the same Renoworks AI platform through 2026. Practical contractor angle: a multi-manufacturer roofer should track which of its primary brands have shipped a visualizer and route homeowner-facing material exploration through the manufacturer-specific tool when one is available, falling back to the generic Hover or AR-overlay flow when not
- **Watch items:** (a) whether GAF, CertainTeed, Atlas, IKO, and Malarkey ship contractor-network-gated AI tools on a similar timeline; (b) whether the AI virtual sales agent pattern (Roof Scout) extends into financing and contract completion in a way that competes with the contractor's own sales process rather than augmenting it; (c) lead-attribution rules — contractors should verify in writing whether a homeowner who completes a manufacturer-hosted experience belongs to the contractor or to the manufacturer's nearest-network referral algorithm

## Visual Proposal & Design Collateral (New — April 2026)
A new category of conversational design tools is emerging that lets non-designers ship on-brand homeowner-facing and commercial deliverables in minutes rather than days. For roofing contractors, this closes a long-standing gap: the estimator has the price right but the deliverable looks like a field-tablet screenshot instead of a brochure.

- **Claude Design (Anthropic Labs, launched April 17, 2026)** — Research-preview conversational design product for Claude Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscribers. Produces one-pagers, pitch decks, interactive prototypes, landing pages, social assets, and marketing materials from natural-language prompts. Reads a team's codebase and design files to build a persistent brand system, then applies it automatically to every subsequent project. Included in paid Claude subscriptions with no additional charge at launch; Enterprise admins must enable it. Relevant roofing use cases: tiered estimate one-pagers, commercial RFP response decks, storm-response flyers, insurance-appeal cover packets, neighborhood trust sheets.
- **Gamma / Beautiful.ai** — Slide-first conversational deck tools; faster than Claude Design for short decks when no brand system is configured, but weaker on one-pagers and printable collateral.
- **Canva AI** — The de-facto fallback for print-heavy collateral (door hangers, yard signs, direct mail) where bleed, trim, and print-ready export matter.
- **Why this category matters for roofing:** Contractors competing for retail (non-insurance) residential and commercial RFP work lose a meaningful share of deals on presentation rather than price. The visual deliverable gap has been one of the larger Phase 3 differentiators the adoption roadmap flagged without a good tool until now. ## Homeowner AI-Assistant Behavioral Benchmarks (April 2026)
A reference set of benchmarks drawn from early-2026 consumer surveys and industry reporting — useful when calibrating which channels deserve investment:
- Roughly 1 in 3 homeowners under 45 have used an AI assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Siri) to find a home-services provider in the past 90 days
- Roughly 41% of surveyed homeowners now trust AI recommendations for local services "as much or more" than personal referrals (up from about 12% in 2024)
- AI-referred leads report meaningfully higher close rates than Google-organic leads, commonly in the 60–75% vs. 25–35% range
- 62% of homeowners act within 30 minutes of receiving an AI recommendation
- Major AI assistants typically shortlist only about 1–2% of local businesses for a given query — concentration is high and visibility compounds quickly once an AEO program is in place
- Post-storm (first 72 hours) AI-assistant queries for local roofers spike sharply; contractors whose visibility program is already running capture that window

## Multi-Location Standardization Pattern
Larger operators (20+ branches, $100M+ revenue) have surfaced a consistent precondition for AI payoff: standardize the operation first so that data flows cleanly, then deploy AI on top. The sequence observed in publicly reported cases from the largest US roofing operators: (1) normalize workflows across branches to eliminate tribal-knowledge variance, (2) put every branch on a single CRM of record with consistent pipeline stages and data entry standards, (3) then layer AI for recruiting triage, branch-level performance variance detection, and contract-to-install cycle-time reduction. Reported outcome at one operator (80+ branches): contract-to-installation window compressed from 41 days to under 20 days, plus recruiting throughput of 250–300 hires/week with AI-assisted screening. Relevance to typical $3–15M roofers: the principle scales down — data quality is the rate-limiting step on AI payoff, not tool selection.

## Commercial Roof Monitoring (New — April 2026)
A category sitting between weather-data services and proactive-maintenance platforms, aimed at commercial property managers and the roofing contractors they retain. The pattern is to ingest a portfolio of insured-roof addresses, watch the weather data continuously, and route an alert plus a recommended response when storm activity crosses a threshold over any address in the portfolio.

- **ShelterGuard.AI (TeamCraft Roofing, expected 2026)** — Commercial-portfolio storm-monitoring platform. Ingests an uploaded portfolio of properties and connects them to a continuous weather-data feed; the open API supports integration with property-management workflows. Pre-storm, the platform highlights the addresses most at risk so crews can stage materials and routes; post-storm, the platform produces an impact list so the contractor can reach affected sites in minutes rather than days. Pattern note for roofers: this is the first roofing-vendor-built example of the same continuous-monitoring loop that homeowner-side carrier AI has been running for two years — the contractor side now has a symmetric tool. Worth watching for general-availability pricing and whether the API exposes Model-Context-Protocol or agent-callable interfaces, which would line it up with the agentic property-intelligence pattern Eagleview Horizon launched in April

## First Public Roofing-Specific AI Prompt Repository (Observed April 2026)
After seven consecutive landscape-monitor scans with no roofing-specific AI prompt repository on GitHub, the first one surfaced in late April 2026: a single-shop public repository (NimbusIQ/nimbusroofing) publishing ten Perplexity-style prompts covering storm intelligence, competitor research, material/technology research, local SEO content ideation, lead qualification, insurance-claim support research, seasonal planning, objection handling, emergency response, and technology evaluation. The shop-specific framing (Dallas-Fort Worth service area, named neighborhood targets, named manufacturer list) is itself a useful pattern: a roofer's prompt library is more effective with the company's actual service area, target keywords, and operating constraints baked in than with generic placeholders. The KRASA AI roofing skills repo already organizes its skills with `config.yml` personalization for this reason, but the public emergence of a peer single-shop library is a signal the open-source roofing-AI category is starting to crystallize.

## Agent Skills as a Distribution Standard (New — late April 2026)
A platform-layer shift surfaced in the second half of April 2026 that changes how a roofing shop receives, installs, and runs the kind of skills this repo authors. Anthropic published the Agent Skills specification as an open standard, opened organization-wide skill management on Team and Enterprise plans, and partnered with Canva, Notion, Figma, and Atlassian to ship pre-built skills out of the box. GitHub shipped a complementary CLI in mid-April that installs agent skills with version pinning and writes provenance metadata (source repository, ref, tree SHA) directly into the SKILL.md frontmatter so the skill carries its origin record wherever it ends up.

- **What's new:** Agent skills are now portable across compatible runtimes rather than locked to a single product. A shop that adopts the KRASA AI roofing skills (or a peer roofing-skills library) can pin to a specific commit, audit which skills are running, and centrally control which crew or department gets which skills. The lock-in question that previously stalled small shops on AI tooling shifts away from the model and toward the orchestration layer
- **Why it matters for roofers:** The roofing-specific skills in this repo (`storm-canvassing-prioritizer`, `insurance-appeal-inspection-report`, `safety-toolbox-talk-generator`, etc.) become a portable artifact. A multi-branch operator can deploy the same vetted skill set across all locations with provenance tracking; a small shop can install the repo's skills against the runtime its team already uses and turn them on per role. Open-standard skills also lower the bar for a shop to author internal skills (its own shop-specific scripts) and run them alongside the public ones
- **Practical contractor angle:** Operators evaluating an AI build-vs-buy decision in 2026 should weight portability of the skill format more heavily than they did in 2025. A skill set that runs only inside one vendor's product creates the same single-vendor lock-in problem the CRM market has spent the last decade unwinding. The KRASA AI repo's per-skill files with frontmatter are aligned with the open-standard pattern — the skills can be installed via the CLI tooling that is now standardizing
- **Watch items:** (a) whether competing model providers (OpenAI, Google, Microsoft) adopt or fork the spec, which will determine whether the skill format is truly cross-runtime or splits into competing dialects; (b) whether roofing-specific skill registries emerge as a category, parallel to how CRM marketplaces and GAF / Owens Corning / CertainTeed contractor-network programs evolved; (c) whether MCP-exposing tools (Eagleview Horizon, ShelterGuard.AI when it ships) get bundled with reference skills that orchestrate them — that combination would make the skill format the de-facto integration layer for vertical roofing AI

## General AI Assistants
- **Claude (Anthropic)** — Proposal writing, email drafting, estimate explanations, customer communication. Claude Opus 4.7 (April 2026) raised supported input image resolution to approximately 2,576 pixels on the long edge — meaningful for roofers because drone and aerial photos that used to be downscaled for analysis can now be processed closer to native resolution, improving hail-spatter and granule-loss detection in the `roof-inspection-report` and `insurance-appeal-inspection-report` workflows. On April 24, 2026 Anthropic opened a public beta of **memory on Claude Managed Agents**, which lets a cloud-hosted agent carry learned context across sessions (user preferences, recurring error patterns, shop-specific conventions) without the operator maintaining memory infrastructure. Published early-adopter results cite high-double-digit reductions in first-pass error rates and 30-ish-percent improvements in document-verification throughput — pattern-relevant for a roofing shop standing up a persistent supplement-review or inbound-triage agent that should not re-learn the shop's playbook every session. In late April 2026 Anthropic also published the **Agent Skills specification as an open standard** with organization-wide skill management for Team and Enterprise plans — see the `Agent Skills as a Distribution Standard` section above for the contractor-side implications
- **ChatGPT (OpenAI)** — Similar general-purpose use cases for content generation and analysis. April 22, 2026 launch of **Workspace Agents** (Codex-powered, shareable within an organization, free research-preview on Business/Enterprise/Edu/Teachers plans through May 6, 2026) makes team-built roofing agents more practical — a small shop can build one inbound-lead triage agent and share it across the sales team without rebuilding it per rep. April 23, 2026 launch of **GPT-5.5** adds native desktop-navigation capabilities — the model can click buttons and type text across desktop applications to execute multi-step workflows, which is relevant for smaller roofing shops whose CRM or quoting platform lacks a usable API: a desktop agent running on top of the existing UI is now viable where an integration-first approach was not. GPT-5.5 also reports token-efficiency gains that reduce the running cost of long-form tasks like supplement drafting and inspection-report generation
